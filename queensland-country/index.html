9 must-do caravan experiences in Queensland Country

The best van holidays aren’t measured by how far you tow, but by the mornings you don’t rush to hitch up.

The Academy Toyota and Jayco Expanda. Photograph by the Australian Offroad Academy. The pictures of each stop are real, and credited underneath.

Queensland Country is built for a caravan. Dams with a site above the water, a falls across the road from the park, cellar doors a short drive from the annex, and one of the state’s great gorge walks at the end of a long dirt-road day.

The list below is an alternative to the usual weekend highlights. Same country. A tow-ball’s point of view. You do not have to do all nine. Somerset, the falls and the Granite Belt is already a rich week. Save Carnarvon and the Gemfields for the trip with time up its sleeve.

    You don’t have to do the lot

    A close week is Somerset, a resupply night in Toowoomba, two nights opposite Queen Mary Falls and two in the Granite Belt. Add Yelarbon if you like a detour with a view, and Boondooma if someone in the van actually fishes.

    Carnarvon Gorge and the Gemfields are the long haul. They are worth it, and they are a different holiday: fuel, an unsealed road, and days you do not spend hitching up. Queensland Country rewards the travellers who unhitch before they are tired.
